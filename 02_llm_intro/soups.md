**1. Vietnamese Phở Gà**  
A fragrant, clear Vietnamese noodle soup featuring simmered chicken broth, soft rice noodles, and tender poached chicken, perfumed with star anise, cinnamon, and charred ginger.  

• Chicken bones and carcass  
• Charred onion and ginger  
• Star anise, cinnamon sticks, cloves  
• Fish sauce and rock sugar  
• Rice noodles (bánh phở)  
• Boneless chicken breast  
• Fresh Thai basil, cilantro, lime wedges  
• Bean sprouts and sliced green onions  

**Steps:**  
1. Roast chicken bones, onion, and ginger over high heat until darkened, then transfer to a large pot with water and bring to a boil.  
2. Skim off foam, then simmer gently for 2–3 hours, adding spices, fish sauce, and sugar toward the end.  
3. Meanwhile, poach chicken breast in simmering water until just cooked, then slice thinly.  
4. Cook rice noodles according to package directions, drain, and divide among bowls. Ladle hot broth over noodles, top with chicken slices, and garnish with fresh herbs, sprouts, and lime.  

**2. Thai Tom Yum Goong**  
Thailand’s iconic hot-and-sour shrimp soup, built on a fragrant base of lemongrass, kaffir lime leaves, and galangal, balanced with chilies, fish sauce, and a splash of lime.  

• Large shrimp, peeled and deveined  
• Lemongrass stalks, bruised  
• Kaffir lime leaves, torn  
• Galangal slices  
• Thai bird’s eye chilies, sliced  
• Fish sauce and lime juice  
• Straw or oyster mushrooms  
• Cilantro roots or stems for garnish  

**Steps:**  
1. In a pot, combine lemongrass, galangal, and kaffir lime leaves with 4 cups of water or stock; bring to a gentle boil.  
2. Add chilies and shrimp, cooking just until the shrimp turn pink and opaque.  
3. Stir in fish sauce and lime juice, adjusting balance to hot-sour preference.  
4. Add mushrooms in the last minute, then remove from heat and sprinkle with cilantro before serving.  

**3. Japanese Miso Soup**  
A daily Japanese staple featuring a clear dashi stock enriched with fermented miso paste, often complemented by silky tofu and umami-rich wakame seaweed.  

• Dashi stock (kombu and bonito flake base)  
• White miso paste  
• Firm tofu, cut into small cubes  
• Dried wakame seaweed, rehydrated  
• Fresh green onions, finely sliced  

**Steps:**  
1. Heat dashi in a small pot over medium heat; just before it reaches a full boil, reduce to low.  
2. Take a ladleful of hot dashi into a small bowl, whisk in the miso paste until smooth, then return the mixture to the pot—avoid boiling miso to preserve flavor.  
3. Add tofu cubes and rehydrated wakame, gently simmering for 2–3 minutes until heated through.  
4. Ladle into bowls and immediately sprinkle with sliced green onions for brightness.
